P6: 1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement
P7: 1.1 What is Antigravity?
P8: Antigravity is an AI-powered GIS disaster management and emergency routing platform. It monitors landslide-prone mountain areas, detects blocked highways, explains why roads are closed, and automatically calculates safe alternative routes for ambulances, supply trucks, and citizens.
P9: It combines 8 key ingredients into one live map: Rainfall sensors + Soil moisture + Mountain slope/terrain + Historical landslide records + Authoritative road maps + Field officer reports + Disaster incidents + High-speed offline routing.
P11: 1.2 The Real-World Problem in North East India
P12: In the mountains of North East India (Assam, Meghalaya, Sikkim, Arunachal Pradesh), monsoon rains trigger massive landslides, flash floods, and road collapses every single year. Critical highways (like NH-6 and NH-10) get completely buried under mud and rock.
P13: During disasters, authorities and citizens face five major headaches:
P14: 1. Sudden Washouts: Roads get blocked suddenly with no advance warning.
P15: 2. Fragmented Information: Information is scattered across police, weather, highway, and district offices.
P16: 3. Opaque Road Closures: Regular apps show a red line for traffic, but nobody knows if the road is just slow or physically destroyed.
P17: 4. Dangerous Navigation: GPS apps try to send ambulances through dangerous mudslides or non-existent mountain tracks.
P18: 5. Community Isolation: Villages get isolated for days because rescue teams cannot find a safe detour.

P66: 7. Road Ingestion & Spatial Validation Rules
P67: Where do our roads come from? Antigravity uses verified vector road data from OpenStreetMap (OSM) and Government GIS portals (Bhuvan/NHAI).
P68: If AI computer vision is used to find candidate roads from satellite images, those candidate roads are quarantined until they pass 5 strict validation tests:
P69: 1. No Building Collisions: Rejects candidate roads that cut through building polygons.
P70: 2. No Unbridged Water Crossings: Rejects roads crossing rivers or gorges unless a verified bridge exists.
P71: 3. Slope Gradient Check: Rejects candidate paths steeper than 28° (which are un-drivable cliffs).
P72: 4. Network Connectivity: Requires candidate roads to snap securely to the existing road network within 15 meters.
P73: 5. Clean Geometry: Guarantees the road is a valid, clean OGC LineString with zero self-intersections.

P88: • Valhalla

P90: Why do we use it? It can calculate routes offline and avoid custom disaster polygons instantly.
P91: How is it used in Antigravity? Ingests the North East road network and computes emergency routes.
P92: What does it produce? Turn-by-turn navigation geometries, distances, and detour paths.
P93: • FastAPI
P94: What is it? Modern, super-fast web framework for Python.
P95: Why do we use it? Handles hundreds of emergency requests per second with automatic data validation.
P96: How is it used in Antigravity? Acts as the conductor: asks PostGIS for blocked roads, tells Valhalla to avoid them, and sends the safe route to the map.
P97: What does it produce? Clean REST API endpoints for the dashboard.
P98: • MapLibre GL
P99: What is it? High-speed interactive map rendering library.

P101: How is it used in Antigravity? Renders the live command dashboard map in the web browser.
P102: What does it produce? Interactive visual map with clickable roads and side panels.
P103: • Docker
P104: What is it? Tool that packages software into isolated containers.
P105: Why do we use it? Ensures the software runs perfectly on any computer or server without install headaches.
P106: How is it used in Antigravity? Runs PostGIS, Valhalla, FastAPI, and Next.js in synchronized containers.
P107: What does it produce? One-click reproducible system deployment (`docker compose up -d`).

P152: 14.2 Top 8 Viva & Review Questions (Crystal-Clear Answers)
P153: Q1: Why use PostGIS instead of standard MySQL or MongoDB?
P154: PostGIS is built specifically for maps. It can check if a road intersects a landslide polygon in 1 millisecond using spatial indexes. Standard databases cannot do fast geometric math.
P155: Q2: Why not just use Google Maps?
P156: Google Maps is a closed black box optimized for speed, not disaster safety. It does not let us inject custom disaster polygons to block dangerous mountain passes, and it cannot run offline in a disconnected control room.
P157: Q3: What does Valhalla do?
P158: Valhalla is our offline routing engine. It holds the road graph in memory and calculates real detours while respecting the blocked areas sent by PostGIS.
P159: Q4: How do you know a road is blocked?
P160: Through verified reports from field officers with GPS photos, highway department feeds, and confirmed landslide events.
P161: Q5: What happens if all roads are cut off?
P162: Antigravity returns 'NO SAFE ROUTE'. It will NEVER draw a fake straight line through a mountain cliff.
P163: Q6: How do you prevent fake roads from crossing buildings or rivers?
P164: Every candidate road must pass 5 PostGIS tests: checking building collisions, slope gradients (<28°), unbridged water, and connectivity to real roads.
P165: Q7: Can this work without internet?
P166: Yes! Valhalla runs on local pre-compiled road tiles on our server. Emergency navigation continues even if the wider internet is down.
P167: Q8: Can this be used for floods or earthquakes?
P168: Yes! Any hazard polygon (flood water, earthquake fissure) can be fed into Antigravity to block affected roads identically.
